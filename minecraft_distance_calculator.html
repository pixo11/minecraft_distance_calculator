How coordinates work

Before any math, one picture. Minecraft's ground is a flat grid seen from above, and every spot has two numbers on it.

Walk towardAxisNumber goes
EastXup (+X)
WestXdown (-X)
SouthZup (+Z)
NorthZdown (-Z)
Watch out: North is smaller Z, not bigger. That is the opposite of a school graph, where up means bigger. In Minecraft, South is "down the page" and has the bigger Z.

Y is height. It goes up and down and plays no part in these distances. The calculator measures along the ground, like a bird flying level.

The middle of the world is X 0, Z 0. Everything else is "how far east or west, and how far north or south" from that point.

The math, step by step

This is the whole engine. Move the two dots (click the map, or type numbers) and every step below recalculates. Press Play to watch the calculation build up in order.

You
Target (a waypoint in the Overworld)

The map shows -500 to 500. Numbers outside that still work in the math, the dot just stops at the edge.

    Why this works: the gap east-west (dx) and the gap north-south (dz) form the two short sides of a right-angled triangle. The straight line from you to the target is the long side. The Pythagorean theorem says long side² = short side² + other short side², so the distance is the square root of dx² + dz². The blue and purple lines in the map are dx and dz, and the orange line is the answer.

    Bearing and direction

    The needle, the degrees and the direction name are three views of one angle. Drag the slider to see how they connect.

    0°
    North

    Where the angle comes from

    bearing = atan2( dx , -dz ) in degrees, then add 360 if it is negative

    A bearing is measured clockwise from North: 0° is North, 90° East, 180° South, 270° West. The function atan2 turns a pair of gaps into an angle. School trigonometry starts at East and turns counter-clockwise, so two swaps are needed: using -dz makes "North" the starting direction (North is negative Z), and putting dx first makes it turn clockwise. atan2 answers between -180° and 180°, so a negative result gets +360 to land between 0° and 360°.

    Where the direction name comes from

    index = round( bearing ÷ 45 ) mod 8

    The eight names sit every 45° in this order: North, North-East, East, South-East, South, South-West, West, North-West. Dividing by 45 and rounding picks the nearest one, so each name owns a 45° slice centered on it (North covers 337.5° to 22.5°). The mod 8 makes 360° wrap back around to North. The shaded slice in the compass above is exactly this rule.

    5

    Travel time

    Time is just distance divided by speed. Type a distance and race different ways of getting there.

    time (seconds) = distance (blocks) ÷ speed (blocks per second)

    Walking is about 4.317 blocks per second and sprinting about 5.612 on flat ground (values from the Minecraft Wiki, editable under "Speeds" on the Calculator page). The calculator rounds to the nearest second and writes it as minutes and seconds: minutes = floor(seconds ÷ 60) and seconds left = seconds mod 60.

    Other ways to travel

    On the Calculator page, the Also show travel by box adds one more time to every waypoint card, for the method you pick: boat, minecart, horse, elytra and more. You can edit its speed if yours differs, and the race above gets a fifth lane for it. These speeds come from the Minecraft Wiki and assume flat ground with no Speed or Slowness effects.

    The Nether lanes cut the distance by 8 first, because the Nether is scaled 1 to 8 (next sections). Real trips also include building a portal and about 4 seconds standing inside it in Survival, so the Nether only wins on longer trips.

    6

    The 8 to 1 Nether scale

    One block you walk in the Nether covers eight blocks of the Overworld. That is why the Nether is the world's shortcut.

    Overworld → Nether: X ÷ 8 and Z ÷ 8 (rounded down) Nether → Overworld: X × 8 and Z × 8

    Only X and Z are scaled. Height (Y) is not. The game rounds down when dividing (floor), so -10.625 becomes -11, and 1.625 becomes 1. The converter below does the same.

    Linking portals

    When you step through a portal, the game takes your converted coordinates and looks for an existing portal nearby. If it finds one it links to it, and if not it builds a new one. According to the Minecraft Wiki the search area is: going Overworld to Nether, a 16-block radius in Java Edition (128 in Bedrock); going Nether to Overworld, a 128-block radius in both. This is why the calculator shows the matching spot: build your portal close to it and it will link where you expect.

    Good to know

    Straight line, not a path. Distances are as the crow flies. Lakes, mountains and ravines make the real walk longer, and the time estimate assumes flat ground with nothing in the way.
    Height is ignored. The full 3D distance would be √(dx² + dy² + dz²). This calculator leaves out dy on purpose, because walking on the ground is what you usually care about.
    Speeds are approximations. Soul sand, ice, water, boats, horses, elytra and speed potions all change them. Pick a travel method on the Calculator page, or edit the speed boxes, to match how you travel.
    Spawn is not always 0, 0. It is usually close. If you know your real spawn coordinates, type them in.
    Portals can drift a little. Because the game links to the nearest portal within its search area, you may arrive a few blocks from the matching spot. Use the numbers as a target, not a pin.
    Your data stays with you. What you type is saved in this browser on this device only, and nothing is sent anywhere. Reset everything clears it, and so does clearing your browser data.
